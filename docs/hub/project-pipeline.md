# Project pipeline: Idea → Prototype → Funding

This guide describes what to capture as a project moves through the Hub pipeline. Progress is not automatic: screening and later reviews help determine a realistic next step. Funding is an opportunity to pursue, not a guaranteed outcome.

## 1. Idea intake

Submit a concise, one-page description using the [project idea template](../../templates/project-idea.md). Capture:

- The problem, who experiences it, and why it matters
- A proposed solution and the expected prototype
- The team, its current skills, and relevant experience
- An initial technical approach and required resources
- Risks, blockers, and the team's suggested next step

Collect only information needed to understand and support the idea. Do not request unnecessary personal information.

## 2. Screening

Review the proposal against four considerations:

1. **Real problem:** Is there a clear problem and an identifiable group affected?
2. **Feasibility:** Is there a credible way to make progress with available time and resources?
3. **Team:** Are the people and skills needed available, or can gaps be addressed?
4. **Clear next step:** Is there a practical action that can test or advance the idea?

Record the date, reviewers or responsible group, concise evidence, questions, outcome, rationale, and next action in the [screening template](../../templates/project-screening.md). Possible outcomes include proceeding to incubation, requesting clarification, suggesting a smaller test, or not proceeding at this time. Do not represent screening as a funding decision.

## 3. Incubation

For projects selected to proceed, record the project stage, agreed scope, team, support needs, and immediate priorities. Confirm who will coordinate follow-up under the Hub's approved arrangements. Keep the project record in the appropriate location under [`projects/`](../../projects/).

## 4. Mentorship

Record the topics for which a team wants guidance, agreed actions, relevant follow-up, and any unresolved questions. Capture only information appropriate for a shared organizational repository; keep private or sensitive discussions out of public project files.

## 5. Milestones

Use the [project milestone template](../../templates/project-milestone.md) to define a result, owner or responsible team member, target date, evidence of completion, dependencies, and blockers. Milestones should be understandable and achievable, and can be revised when circumstances change.

## 6. Monthly check-ins

At each monthly check-in, note the date, progress against milestones, evidence or demo, current blockers, support needed, decisions, and next actions with owners and target dates. Update the project stage and next milestone. Keep the record factual; distinguish completed work from plans.

## 7. Showcase or demo

When a project has a suitable result to present, record the demo or showcase context, the problem addressed, what works, limitations, feedback, and follow-up actions. A showcase opportunity does not itself imply endorsement or funding.

## 8. Funding

If a project is ready to seek support, document the resource or funding need, intended use, estimated amount or scope where known, relevant opportunity, submission owner, deadline, and outcome when confirmed. Possible routes include grants, sponsorships, competitions, or related opportunities. Coordinate proposals and financial handling through the applicable approved ACES process, including the Financial Secretary and Grants Desk where relevant. Never publish private financial or account details.

## Keeping records useful

Use the [project documentation guidance](../../projects/README.md) and keep records factual, proportionate, and useful for the next team. Do not invent project names, members, decisions, funding, or outcomes.
